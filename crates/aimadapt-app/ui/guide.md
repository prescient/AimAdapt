# AimAdapt user guide

This guide is for AimAdapt 0.1.0. It covers every page of the app, the trainer window, how ratings work, and what each built-in scenario trains.

The app has four pages, in the sidebar: **Today** (what to play now), **Scenarios** (everything you can play, and how you're doing on each), **Progress** (how you're doing overall) and **Settings**. Help is at the bottom of the sidebar, and every page has a Help link to its own section. When the window is 1180 pixels wide or less, the sidebar shows only icons: point at one to see its name. On the Help page, the box at the top of the contents list filters the guide. Only sections that match stay in the list and on the page. The list marks the section you're in, and scrolls on its own when the window is short.

## Getting started {#start}

1. **Set up your aim.** Open Settings. Pick the game you play, type your sensitivity exactly as it is in that game, and set your mouse DPI and field of view. If KovaaK's is installed, **Match my KovaaK's settings** copies your latest KovaaK's run instead.
2. **Choose a display mode.** Borderless full screen is the default. See [Settings](#help/settings) for exclusive full screen, a window, and a smaller play area on a big monitor.
3. **Play today's workout.** Today shows today's day of the Core Week program. Press **Start session** and the trainer opens over the app.
4. **Expect a calibration run.** Your first adaptive run on each scenario finds your level as you play, so it gets harder and easier along the way. After that, every run plays at one difficulty. A scenario you come back to after several days away gets a calibration run again.
5. **Check your progress.** Progress shows your skill profile and how your week went. Each scenario's page on Scenarios breaks it down: where you miss, how fast you react, how steady your tracking is, with replays of your recent runs.

Train most days and take Sunday off. Half an hour a day is a sound default, and nothing suggests it is a ceiling: in the largest study of aim training, gains on a single task kept growing up to about an hour of it in a day, with most of the benefit reached within 30 to 60 minutes. Training on more days mattered more than the length of any one day. Your daily goal is on [Settings › Training](#help/settings). Day-to-day changes under about 5% are mostly luck. Look at the trend over a week or two.

## Today {#today}

Today is the home page: what to play now and how the week is going.

- **Streak and today's time** at the top: the days in a row you've trained, and today's active time against your daily goal, which you set on Settings › Training.
- **Today's workout:** today's day of the Core Week program, with each drill's block (warm-up, main, secondary, maintenance), its runs and your current 85% rating. The bar shows how many of today's runs you've done. The button says **Start session**, **Continue session** if you've done part of it, or **Repeat session** once it's done. **Something else** opens Scenarios on Browse. A drill's name opens it on Scenarios. On a rest day it tells you what's next. The line under the title names your adaptive mix and links to Settings › Training, where you change it.
- **Last session:** what your last session in the trainer changed. Each scenario shows its 85% rating before and after the session and the change with its likely range, grey when zero is inside the range. The line under the title has the day, the active time, the runs and any breakthroughs. **Summary** opens the [session summary](#help/session). The card appears once you have played a run in the trainer.
- **This week:** each day's minutes in the program, coloured done, partial or missed. **Whole week** opens the Program tab on Scenarios, with every day's drills.
- **Favorites:** the scenarios and workouts you've starred (up to six), with **Play** on scenarios and **Start session** on workouts.
- **Recently played:** your last five scenarios or workouts, with your current figure and **Play** on scenarios or **Start session** on workouts. Play starts an adaptive run. KovaaK's scenarios have no button here unless AimAdapt can play them (then it says **Play in AimAdapt**); the rest you play in KovaaK's.
- **Worth knowing:** the top note: the most important one from your skill profile and history (see Worth knowing under [Progress](#help/progress)). When your least improved skill is the top note, it links to that skill's scenarios on Browse. **All notes** opens the full list on Progress › Skills. With no notes, the card isn't shown.

## Session summary {#session}

When you close the trainer after playing at least one run, the app opens the session summary: what the session you just played changed. It is also behind **Summary** on Today's Last session card. A session is the same as on Progress › Time: a new one starts after a 15-minute break, and KovaaK's runs in the same sitting count toward its length.

- **Active time, runs and breakthroughs** are at the top. Active time is the time inside runs. Runs counts the trainer's runs and says how many more you played in KovaaK's.
- **Scenarios** has one row for each scenario you played, in the order you first played them.
  - **85% rating** is your rating before the session and after it. Before means the last rating you had on the same version of the scenario, and the line under the change says which day that was. A scenario rated for the first time shows the new rating alone.
  - **Change** is how much better or worse the rating is, with its likely range. A grey change has zero inside its range, so it may be noise. One session moves a rating a little. The trend is on the scenario's page.
  - A program drill also shows its change since the same day of the program the time before, usually a week ago, when that is a different day from the one above.
  - **Breakthroughs** counts the runs that held a harder level than any run before (see [Adaptive mode](#help/adaptive)).
  - A scenario you played only in standard mode shows the best score of the session.
- **Worth knowing** is one note from the scenario you played most, the one most worth acting on next. Its link opens that scenario's insights, where every note is.

**Back** returns to the page you were on. When that page was Today, the **Today** button is the way back. If you pressed **V** or **I** on the trainer's results screen, the app opens that replay or scenario's page and the summary stays on Today's card. After a test run from the scenario or workout editor the app stays on the editor. If the trainer closed with an error, the app stays where it was and shows the error.

## Scenarios {#scenarios}

Scenarios has three tabs across the page: two lists, **Browse** and **Workouts**, and **Program**, which shows the week's plan (see below). Each scenario and workout opens on its own page; **Scenarios ·** and the list's name, at the top of that page, take you back to the tab where you left it. The Scenarios link in the sidebar opens the tab you were last on. A trainer scenario's or workout's page shows the adaptive mix in use at the top, with a link to Settings › Training.

- **Browse:** every scenario, played or not: the trainer's, and your KovaaK's scenarios (every `.sce` file in your KovaaK's and Workshop folders, plus any you've played in KovaaK's). A KovaaK's scenario's skill is its aim type (Clicking, Tracking or Target switching) and its tags are the game and weapon tags in its file. Filter by **Type** (AimAdapt, KovaaK's, or KovaaK's that play here: the ones AimAdapt's trainer can play), **Skill**, **Played** (played or not played yet), or pick tags (for example *tracking* and *smooth*) to show only scenarios that have all of them. Each tag shows how many scenarios have it; **N more** shows the rest of the tags. The **Yours** button next to **Favorites** shows only the scenarios you made or copied with Copy & edit, and it appears once you have some. Once you've played anything, Browse opens with the most recently played first, grouped by day (Today, Yesterday, then weekdays and dates), and scenarios you haven't played at the end. Before that it opens grouped by skill. Set the **Played** filter to Played to see only what you've played, here or in KovaaK's. KovaaK's rows are scenarios you played in KovaaK's: their page shows your scores, but you play them in KovaaK's.
- **Workouts:** the built-in workouts and yours, with how many scenarios and runs each has and about how long it takes. Filter by **Made by** (built-in or you). The Core Week's daily sessions aren't listed here. They're on Today and the Program tab.
- **Program:** the plan for the week. It isn't a list, so it has no search or filters. There is a card for each day, Monday to Sunday, with today marked. A training day shows its focus, how many drills, runs and minutes it has, each drill (its block, its name as a link to the scenario, its runs, and "standard" for a drill played in standard mode), how many of its runs you've done, and its session button: **Start session**, **Continue session** if you've done part of it, or **Repeat session** once it's done. A missed day can be made up later in the week. A rest day has no button. The weak-spots day says its drills come from the skills you have trained least and improved least. The top of the tab has the program's name and description, how many days are done this week, and links to the guide and to your results on Progress › Skills. A drill's page links back to the Program tab when you came from it. See [The Core Week program](#help/program).

**Searching and sorting:** the search on each tab finds every word you type in a name, skill, tag or description, so *wide flick* finds Wide Flicks and anything tagged both. Click a column heading to sort by it, and again to reverse it. Sorting by Skill, Type, Made by, Rating or score or Last played puts a heading over each group. Ratings only compare within one kind (kill pace, speed, tracking speed or size), so Rating or score sorts ratings by kind, best first. Scenarios that only have a score come next, and those with neither come last. Lists show 100 rows at a time: **Show more** or **Show all** at the bottom shows the rest. Each tab keeps its own search, filters and sort while the app is open. **Clear filters** shows the whole tab again.

Each row has the scenario's skill and tags, one **Rating or score** figure (your 85% rating on an AimAdapt scenario, or your best score where it has no rating, as for KovaaK's scenarios), how many runs you've played, when you last played it, and **Play** (an adaptive run) or, for a workout, **Start session**. A narrow window drops the Runs, Type and Skill columns, in that order, so **Play** stays in view. A KovaaK's row marked *plays here* has **Play in AimAdapt**, which plays its file in AimAdapt's trainer (see [Playing KovaaK's scenarios](#help/kovaaks-play)); other KovaaK's scenarios you play in KovaaK's. Reading your KovaaK's files takes a moment the first time Scenarios opens; the count line says so while it does.

**Favorites and hiding:** the star on a row, or at the top of a scenario's or workout's page, adds it to your favorites. **Favorites** next to the search shows only your favorites, and they show on Today. **Hide**, on an item's page, keeps something off the lists, for example an old KovaaK's scenario you don't play any more. Its runs aren't deleted. Once you've hidden something, a **Hidden** tab lists it, and **Show again** brings it back.

**Playing:** **Play** always means an adaptive run of one scenario. On a row, **Play** starts one. On a scenario's page, **Play** is at the top, with a small line under it saying what it does (for example "adaptive · 60 s"), and **Standard run** beside it plays the scenario as written; **Copy & edit** (or **Edit** for your own) opens the [scenario editor](#help/edit). A workout's page lists its runs, with **Start session** and **Copy & edit** (see [Workouts](#help/workout)). **New workout** and **New scenario** start empty ones. **Playing with** shows the settings profile the trainer will use; it opens Settings.

While the trainer is open, the page says so and the start buttons are disabled. Each run is saved when its timer ends, so quitting partway through a workout keeps the runs you finished.

**A scenario's page** has one header and, once you've played it, three tabs under it: **Overview**, **Insights** and **Runs**. Each tab has its own address, so Back returns to the tab you were on. The header holds the scenario's name, description and facts, the star and **Hide**, **Copy & edit**, **Standard run** and **Play**. A scenario with adaptive runs also has a line of figures there: your latest 85% rating with its likely range, and the best you've held (for example *85% speed 0.62 s (likely 0.58–0.66 s) · best held 0.55 s*). Before the first run the page has no tabs.
- **Overview:** up to four key figures (targets hit, time on target, reaction, flick speed, or for switching kills a minute and acquire time), the rating by run with its likely range, and **Last run**: how the rating moved during your latest run. Under them are your latest five runs with their kind and **Replay** links (**All N runs** opens the Runs tab) and the first three notes from **What to work on** (a link opens the rest on the Insights tab).
- **Insights:** **What to work on**, plain-language notes from the figures below, then what your runs in the trainer recorded. Every run stores each target, each shot, tracking samples 30 times a second and the aim trace, so these come from what you actually did:
  - **Speed vs. hit rate:** your chance of success at each difficulty. You're reliable (95%) at one end and it's a coin flip (50%) at the other, and the 85% mark is your rating. The one exception is a rating held at the scenario's easiest or hardest setting: the rating stops there, and the mark shows where your curve sits past it. A steep drop means you're consistent up to your limit; a gentle one means misses creep in early.
  - **Speed vs. difficulty:** time to kill against how hard each flick was (distance and size), as a Fitts' law fit. Throughput, in bits per second, is your flick speed corrected for difficulty.
  - **By flick direction** and **By flick distance:** hit rate and median time in eight directions and five distance bands, to find a weak side.
  - **Where shots land:** your shots relative to the target, lined up with the flick, so you can see overshooting, stopping short, or a vertical bias.
  - **Reaction time:** how long before your crosshair starts moving toward a new target, as a histogram. It counts flicks of 3° or more, since on a tiny correction "started moving" hardly means anything. The Reaction column in the runs table counts the same flicks.
  - **Tracking:** time on target by target speed, how long you take to recover after the target changes direction, whether you trail or lead it, your unbroken stretches on target (half your time on target came in stretches at least this long), and centering (how far from the center you sit while on target).
  - **Within a run:** success in 10-second segments, which shows slow starts or fading late.
- **Runs:** two cards.
  - **Planned against actual:** for each kind of run (At your level, Stretch and Easy), how many you've played, the chance of success the trainer planned, how often you succeeded, and the gap in points. A gap is marked "above plan" or "below plan" only when it is bigger than luck explains for that many targets, and never under five points. Anything smaller is greyed. A marked gap means the trainer's estimate was off for that kind of run: "below plan" on stretch runs says they were harder for you than your rating predicted. Calibration runs have no plan and aren't counted. Older runs didn't record their length, so they are left out, and the card says how many.
  - **Runs:** your latest 30 runs with their kind (Standard, Calibration, At your level, Stretch or Easy), rating, hit rate, flick speed, reaction and time on target, the workout they were part of, and a **Replay** link to watch each one again (see [Replay](#help/replay)). **Show all** lists every run. A **Breakthrough** tag beside a rating marks a run that held your goal at a harder level than any earlier run of that version of the scenario. A greyed rating is from before you changed the scenario's settings, so it isn't compared with the others (see [Adaptive mode](#help/adaptive)).

**A KovaaK's scenario's page** has a header with its name, tags and the star and **Hide**, and, once you've played it in KovaaK's, two tabs. **Overview** has **Play in AimAdapt** when the file plays here, your score history with its chart, and **What the data says**. **Runs** has the table of your KovaaK's runs. It has no Insights tab, since KovaaK's files don't record shots or reaction.

**Reaction time and lifetime ratings.** A lifetime rating includes your reaction time, which practice barely changes. A note splits the rating into your reaction and the part you control. That part improves by more than the rating shows: going from 0.80 s to 0.62 s is 23% on the rating, but with a 0.22 s reaction the part you control fell 31%.

**KovaaK's scenarios** show their score history: personal best, average, accuracy and improvement, every score over time with your bests marked, a trend and any plateau, and every run. Changes to your sensitivity show as markers on the chart, so you can tell a settings change from a real gain. Restarted runs (much shorter than usual) never count as bests. KovaaK's stats files don't record shots, reaction or tracking, so these scenarios have no Insights or replays. Standard-mode runs of a trainer scenario are on its own page, with their score in the runs table. Some KovaaK's scenarios can also be played in AimAdapt's trainer: their page then has **Play in AimAdapt** (see [Playing KovaaK's scenarios](#help/kovaaks-play)).

## In the trainer {#trainer}

The trainer is a separate game window. It opens over the app window: centered across it with the tops level, or full screen on the app's monitor.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Mouse | Aim. The cursor is locked to the window while it has focus, and locks again by itself when you come back from another window. |
| Left click | Fire. Some scenarios fire while you hold it, or fire all the time. Clicking also locks the cursor again. |
| R | Restart the current run. After a run: play it again. |
| Space | After a run: play it again, or go to the next run in a workout. |
| V | After a run: close the trainer and open the run's replay in the app. |
| I | After a run: close the trainer and open the scenario's page in the app. |
| Esc | Quit the trainer and return to the app. |
| F2 | Show or hide the FPS counter. |

**On screen:**
- **Top left:** on a calibration run, "Calibration run" with your rating ("85%: 0.62 s"). On every other run there are two lines: the kind of run with its difficulty, then what to expect. A stretch run reads "Stretch run: 0.52 s" over "About 6 in 10 expected", an easy run "Easy run: 0.71 s" over "About 19 in 20 expected", and a run at your level "At your level: 0.60 s" over "Goal 85%". A calibration run after a long break also says how many days it has been.
- **Top center:** the time left.
- **Top right:** your success rate so far (named for what's counted: "Hit rate", "On target", "On pace" or "Finished in time") beside what it is measured against. That is the goal, or on a stretch or easy run what the plan expects ("about 6 in 10 expected"). Under it are kills and kills a minute. Kill-pace scenarios also show how long you have for the next kill.
- **Targets:** timed targets show a bar that empties as their time runs out, and change color by wave so newer ones stand out (three red, then three blue, then red again on Timed Grid). Switching targets show a health bar.

**After a run** the results show your rating and its likely range, your success rate, accuracy, kills, timeouts and average time to kill. Once a scenario has a rating, a line also says how the run went. A run at your level is judged against the 85% goal: you held it, you reached it in a run too short to count, or you came in below. A stretch or easy run is judged against its own plan: "As planned", "Above plan" or "Below plan", with what the plan expected. "As planned" allows for the luck of a run that length and never less than five points either way, so a short run has more room than a long one. A stretch run that holds 85% says so. The line only describes the run: every run counts toward your rating in the same way. A breakthrough gets a banner (see [Adaptive mode](#help/adaptive)).

Under the figures is a small chart of your last ten ratings on the scenario (scores for a standard run), oldest on the left, with this run marked in green. Up is always better, so on a scenario rated in seconds the shortest time is at the top. Runs of an older version of the scenario are left out, and the chart appears from the second run. Below it is one note from your recent runs, the one most worth acting on next. Every note is on the scenario's page. **V** closes the trainer and opens this run's replay, and **I** opens the scenario's page. In a workout both end the workout where it is. When you close the trainer, the app opens the [session summary](#help/session). On a small window the chart and the note are left out when they don't fit, so the keys always stay on the screen.

The last run of a workout also lists the workout: one line for each scenario. A scenario you played once shows that run. One you played several times shows how many runs, with the rating you ended on and the rating you had before the workout, or your best score for standard runs.

## Adaptive mode {#adaptive}

Adaptive mode rates you on one setting per scenario, in real units, and keeps each run near the level you hit about 85% of the time. Standard mode plays the scenario exactly as written and scores points instead.

**What each scenario is rated on:**

| Rating | Units | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target lifetime | seconds | Timed clicking | "0.62 s": you hit 85% of targets that last 0.62 s. Lower is better. |
| Kill pace | seconds between kills | Grids, untimed flicks, speed switching | "0.80 s": 85% of your kills come within 0.8 s of the one before. Lower is better. |
| Target size | radius in meters | Precision drills | "0.085 m": the smallest target you hit (or stay on) 85% of the time. Lower is better. |
| Target speed | degrees per second across your view | Tracking, strafe clicking, switching | "48.5 °/s": you stay on target 85% of the time at that speed. Higher is better. |

Target speed is in degrees per second because that's how fast your hand has to turn. A target moving at a fixed speed in meters per second crosses your view faster when it's close and slower when it's far, so on scenarios with a range of distances the figure is the speed at the average distance.

**What counts as a success:**
- **Clicking:** a target destroyed before it times out, with at most one miss on it. Spraying clicks doesn't count, and a timeout is a failure.
- **Kill pace:** a kill within the pace of the one before. Each full stretch of that length without a kill is a failure, and the late kill that ends it doesn't count either. When clicking, a kill after more than one miss doesn't count.
- **Tracking:** each 2-second segment is scored by the share of time your crosshair is on the target. On Stability Switch, Switch Track and their variants, only time while a target is damaged and not yet healed counts, so moving to the next target isn't held against you.
- **Finished in time:** on Unbroken Strafes and Unbroken Float, each target needs a stretch of beam and heals fast when you slip off. Finishing it before it times out is a success, so only unbroken tracking counts.

**Calibration run, then one difficulty per run.** Your first adaptive run on a scenario is a calibration run: it changes the difficulty as you play to find your level. Every run after that plays at one value for the whole run, shown when it starts, and your rating updates as you play. Each run is one of three kinds:

| Run | Chance of success | Balanced | Steady | Push |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| At your level | 80–90% | 65% | 85% | 50% |
| Stretch | 55–72% | 20% | 5% | 35% |
| Easy | 93–97% | 15% | 10% | 15% |

The mix (Steady, Balanced or Push) is one setting for every profile, on [Settings › Training](#help/settings). A stretch run never follows another stretch run, or a run more than 10 points below the goal. Before a run the trainer names its kind and what to expect, for example "Stretch run: 0.52 s" and "About 6 in 10 expected". Afterwards a stretch or easy run is judged against that plan, and a run at your level against the 85% goal (see [In the trainer](#help/trainer)).

**Several runs in a row.** When a workout plays a scenario more than once in a row, the kind of each run follows a plan built from your mix instead of a random draw. The first run is at your level. Stretch runs sit in the middle, never last and never two in a row. Easy runs come at the end. Five runs on Balanced go: at your level, at your level, stretch, at your level, easy. The shares are rounded to the number of runs, so a short block has fewer kinds: on Balanced and Push a block needs three runs for a stretch run and four for an easy one, and on Steady it needs ten and five. A planned stretch run still becomes a run at your level if the run before it was more than 10 points below the goal. A run played on its own picks its kind at random from the mix.

**After a break.** If you leave a scenario for a few days, your old rating is only a rough guide to where you are now, so the next run is a calibration run again. That happens after three days away if the rating was still settling, and after about five and a half if it was well settled. The run starts from your old rating and adjusts target by target, and the screen says why ("9 days since your last run, finding your level again"). When it ends your rating is updated, and runs go back to one difficulty. A calibration run can't be a breakthrough, and your best held stays as it was.

**Breakthroughs.** Holding 85% for a whole run at a value harder than your best is a breakthrough. The trainer announces it, and it becomes your new best held. A run of 30 targets or more holds the goal at 85% itself. A shorter run needs more, because a few lucky hits can look like skill: all 10 of 10, or 19 of 20. A tracking segment counts as three targets, so a tracking run of 20 seconds or more needs just 85%. A run with fewer than 10 targets or segments never counts. This rule got stricter in November 2026. A best you held before then stays as it is.

**How the rating moves.** The rating is an estimate with an uncertainty, much like a chess rating. A surprising result moves it more than an expected one: succeeding on a stretch run counts for a lot, failing on one costs little. The uncertainty grows with the days since you last played, so after a break your rating moves faster again.

**What 85% means.** The rating is the value you really hit 85% of the time, slips included. A slip is a miss that has nothing to do with difficulty, like a misclick or a moment of lost focus, and the trainer allows for 4% of them. Until November 2026 the rating was the 85% point before that allowance, so your real chance of success at the rating was about 83.6%. Saved ratings were converted once, when the app first opened your history, so each reads about 1 to 3% easier (how much depends on how steeply your success falls off) and nothing was reset. Your charts and changes stay on one scale. A rating held at a scenario's easiest or hardest setting stayed there, and best held figures did not change, because they are values you actually played at.

The estimate also covers how consistent you are, meaning how quickly your success falls off as a drill gets harder. One run can't settle that, so the rating carries what it has learned about it from run to run. That keeps a steady player's rating from wobbling. The cost is a slower answer to a real change: in tests with a simulated player who improves by 10%, a little under half of the gain shows after one run and about nine tenths after five.

**Ratings belong to one version of a scenario.** Changing anything that affects play (speed, size, timers, movement) starts the rating over with a calibration run, because the old rating isn't on the same scale. Changing only the name or description keeps it. Insights and program progress follow the same rule: your best, your best held, the rating chart, the notes and every gain use only runs of the version you last played. The Runs table still lists every run, with ratings from older versions greyed.

## Built-in scenarios {#builtins}

There are 29 built-ins across nine sub-skills, at least two for each. In the Core Week program, most drills swap week by week with a variant that trains the same sub-skill.

**Static clicking**

| Scenario | What it is | Rated on |
|---|---|---|
| Timed Flicks | One target at a time that times out if you're too slow. | Target lifetime |
| Timed Wide Flicks | Timed targets far from the last one. Commit to big flicks. | Target lifetime |
| Timed Micro Flicks | Small timed targets close to the last one. | Target lifetime |
| Wide Flicks | One small untimed target at a time, always far from the last. | Kill pace |
| Micro Flicks | Tiny targets next to the last one, each up for 1.5 s. Precision, not reaction speed. | Target size |
| Static Grid | Three still targets; a new one appears as each goes. | Kill pace |
| Timed Grid | Three targets, each on its own 2 s timer. Clear the oldest first. | Kill pace |
| Shrink Reflex | Targets that shrink and vanish after 1.2 s. | Target lifetime |

**Dynamic and linear clicking**

| Scenario | What it is | Rated on |
|---|---|---|
| Timed Strafe Clicks | A strafing body that times out. Read the strafe and click. | Target lifetime |
| Strafe Clicks | A strafing body with a fixed 2 s timer. | Target speed |
| Hop Clicks | A strafing body that eases into turns and jumps. | Target lifetime |
| Linear Clicks | Targets gliding side to side in straight lines. Lead and click. | Target lifetime |
| Angled Glides | Targets gliding in straight lines at any angle. | Target lifetime |

**Tracking**

| Scenario | What it is | Rated on |
|---|---|---|
| Precise Track | A small ball drifting slowly. Stay centered on it. | Target size |
| Depth Drift | A small ball that drifts and comes closer and backs away. | Target speed |
| Strafe Track | A strafing body. The beam is always on, so only your aim counts. | Target speed |
| Close Strafe Track | A body strafing close to you with quick reversals. | Target speed |
| Hop Strafe Track | A body strafing with realistic turns and jumps. | Target speed |
| Unbroken Strafes | A strafing body that heals fast when you slip off. Finish each within 4 s. | Target speed (finished in time) |
| Control Track | A ball moving in long, smooth curves across a wide area. | Target speed |
| Arc Track | A ball that turns gradually, so it travels in curves. | Target speed |
| Float Track | A large ball drifting in every direction. Hold fire. | Target speed |
| Unbroken Float | A drifting ball that heals fast when you slip off. Finish each within 5 s. | Target speed (finished in time) |

**Switching**

| Scenario | What it is | Rated on |
|---|---|---|
| Speed Switch | Four nearly still targets that melt in a short burst. Kill one, snap to the next. | Kill pace |
| Wide Speed Switch | Five targets spread wide around you. | Kill pace |
| Switch Track | Three moving targets that each take half a second of beam and heal when you leave. | Target speed, while on a kill |
| Hop Switch | Three strafing, jumping bodies. | Target speed, while on a kill |
| Stability Switch | Three strafing targets that each need a long, steady beam. | Target speed, while on a kill |
| Depth Stability Switch | Like Stability Switch, with targets that come closer and back away. | Target speed, while on a kill |

## The Core Week program {#program}

Core Week is a six-day plan of about 29 minutes a day, built around reps rather than variety.

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Static clicking |
| Tuesday | Reactive tracking |
| Wednesday | Switching |
| Thursday | Precise and dynamic clicking |
| Friday | Smooth and precise tracking |
| Saturday | Weak spots: drills for the sub-skills you've trained least and improved least |
| Sunday | Rest |

Each day has a warm-up (one drill, two standard runs), two main drills of five reps, two secondary drills of four, and a maintenance drill from another skill group (three reps). Most drills alternate week by week with a variant that trains the same sub-skill, for example Strafe Track one week and Hop Strafe Track the next. The training day starts at 4 AM, so a late-night session counts toward the day it started on.

Saturday picks its skills in this order: skills you haven't trained come first, then skills with fewer than three sessions (fewest first), then the rest by their change since you started, smallest first. A change measured on fewer than three sessions is mostly noise, which is why it isn't used earlier. This order changed in November 2026, so Saturday's drills may differ from before.

Today shows today's workout. **Scenarios › Program** shows the whole week: every day's drills, with a start button on each day. **Progress › Skills** shows the results: your skill profile, how each drill moves across its reps, and how each day went. Start buttons are only on Today and the Program tab.

## Workouts {#workout}

A workout is a list of scenarios, each with a number of runs and a mode (standard or adaptive), played back to back. When a scenario has several adaptive runs, they follow a planned order: the first at your level, any stretch runs in the middle and any easy runs last (see [Adaptive mode](#help/adaptive)). Restarting a run keeps its kind.

Built-in workouts:
- **Quick Warm-up:** five easy minutes: two standard runs to loosen up, then three adaptive ones.
- **Daily Fundamentals:** about eight minutes of clicking, tracking and switching, all adaptive.
- **Clicking Focus:** timed flicks at every distance, plus reaction and moving targets.
- **Tracking Focus:** reactive strafes, smooth tracking and switching.
- **Core Week: Monday** to **Core Week: Friday:** the program's days. They're played from Today and Scenarios › Program, so they aren't in the Workouts list.

Workouts have their own tab on Scenarios.

**Editing a workout:** add scenarios, set runs and mode for each, and reorder them with the arrows. **Save & start** saves it and plays it straight away. Built-ins are copied before editing, so the originals stay as they are. **Cancel** throws away your changes and goes back to the workout's page.

## Scenario editor {#edit}

The editor is a form with every scenario setting, grouped as in the file format: about, timing, weapon, targets, spawn area, movement, scoring, adaptive and arena. Each field explains itself, and values are checked as you type. Anything that needs fixing is listed under **Fix before saving**. Sections that hold only default values start folded, with "defaults" beside the name. The first section, with the scenario's name, is always open. Click a section's name to open or fold it. It stays as you left it while you edit.

- **Built-ins are copied before editing.** Your copy is saved in your own scenarios folder.
- **Save & play adaptive** saves and starts an adaptive run. **Save** goes back to the scenario's page; **Cancel** does too and throws away your changes.
- **Changing settings starts the rating over** with a calibration run (see [Adaptive mode](#help/adaptive)).
- Scenario files are TOML, so you can also edit them in a text editor. SCENARIOS.md in the project documents every field.

## Progress {#progress}

Progress shows how you're doing across all your scenarios, in three tabs.

**Skills**
- **Key figures:** days done this week in the program, minutes, your overall change since you started each drill, and your biggest gain over the last seven days. The biggest gain is shown with its range, and only when it is outside its own noise.
- **Skill profile:** the change in each of the nine sub-skills, each group and overall, measured from the end of your first day on each drill. Every change has its likely range (one standard deviation either side, built from the ratings' own ranges), drawn as a thin line on the bar and as a ± figure. A grey bar or figure has zero inside its range, so the change may be noise. Changes from runs saved before ranges were stored show no range.
- **Worth knowing:** every note, most important first: your least improved skill, a plateau, warming up first, repeating a scenario before you switch, skills you haven't trained in a week, a change of settings, and what Saturday's weak-spots day will play. Today shows only the first. A note appears only while it applies. A least improved skill is named once two skills have three sessions each, and the skill profile says how many sessions are still to go.
- **Reps and carry-over:** a **By drill** summary first: how much of a drill's rating carries from one session to the next, averaged over its latest sessions. It shows after three sessions of a drill, and says how many more each other drill needs. Then each session: your first rep against your last rep of each drill, and how much of the previous session's rating carried over to the next, with ranges and greyed figures when zero is inside the range.
- **This week:** the results for each Core Week day: done, partial or missed, with runs and minutes, and each day's main drills compared with the same day last week. The comparison has its range, and is greyed and marked "within noise" when zero is inside it. A day counts as done at 75% of its runs. Skills has no start buttons: **The plan** opens the Program tab, where you start a day or make up a missed one.
- **Planned against actual:** at the bottom, the table from a scenario's Runs tab for all your scenarios together: how often you succeeded on runs at your level, stretch runs and easy runs, beside the chance the trainer planned. A gap is marked only when it is bigger than luck explains for that many targets. Older runs that didn't record their length are left out (see Runs under [Scenarios](#help/scenarios)).

**Benchmarks from KovaaK's** lists your best scores on the Voltaic Season 5 benchmark scenarios, read from your KovaaK's stats files. Each opens the scenario on Scenarios. AimAdapt's own scenarios aren't here: they're rated on their own pages and on Skills. If you haven't played any of them in KovaaK's, the tab says so and links to Settings › KovaaK's, where the stats folder is set up.

**Time** shows when and how much you train, counting KovaaK's runs and trainer runs together: totals (active time, session time, days trained, days the daily goal was met), daily time for the last seven days, a calendar of daily time (use the arrows to change month) and every session newest first. **More about when you train** opens four more cards: the last seven days as session blocks, time by day of the week, runs by hour of day, and active against idle time. It starts closed, and stays open or closed as you use the page. How time is counted is in [Settings › Training](#help/settings).

## Playing KovaaK's scenarios {#kovaaks-play}

AimAdapt is learning to play your own KovaaK's scenarios (`.sce` files) in its trainer, closely enough that scores compare with KovaaK's. It reads the file from your KovaaK's or Workshop folder and never changes it.

**What plays so far:** most scenarios with hitscan weapons, on KovaaK's newer (JSON) and older (Reflex) maps, with bots that stand still or move, and helper bots that push the targets around (bots on your own team, which you can't see or shoot). That includes all 34 Voltaic S5 Novice benchmarks. Bots may carry a weapon if they are told not to aim, because such a bot never fires in KovaaK's, and your own character profile may make you invincible, since nothing in AimAdapt hurts you. Bots that blow themselves up play too: a still ball whose weapon ability sends out a projectile that goes off inside it, so a target you don't shoot in time goes by itself. Pushes that do damage or vary in strength, long swings, projectiles, bots that aim and fire at you (unless the scenario makes you invincible) and the rest come later.

**Playing one:** on Scenarios › Browse, pick **Type › KovaaK's that play here** and press **Play in AimAdapt** on a row, or open a KovaaK's scenario. If AimAdapt can play it, its page has a card with a **Play in AimAdapt** button. If the file is there but uses something AimAdapt can't play yet, the page says what. Imported scenarios play in standard mode on the scenario's own map, with its bots, weapon, time limit and scoring, including the accuracy multiplier (some scenarios multiply the score by your accuracy or its square root). If the scenario locks the field of view to a range, your FOV is kept inside it for that run. A scenario with its own time scale (slow motion or sped up) plays at that speed, and its timer counts scenario seconds. Bots appear where the scenario's spawn points and areas put them, pushed out of any wall they'd overlap, as in KovaaK's. Each kind of bot starts at its own height above its spawn point and uses its own spawn offsets. An offset runs along the map's axes (its forward, right and up) however the spawn point is turned, as in KovaaK's. A scenario saved by a KovaaK's older than 2.0.5.0 has its offsets turned a quarter, and AimAdapt reads them that way. The older offset settings (`SpawnXOffset`, `SpawnYOffset` and `VerticalSpawnOffset`) are read too, but a character that sets them together with the newer ones doesn't play, because how KovaaK's adds the two wasn't measured. Your own character's spawn offsets move where you start by the same rule (the older settings were measured in KovaaK's with test maps). A player character that falls and has an older vertical offset doesn't play, because where it lands wasn't measured. A bot that appears with a floor just below its feet, within its character's step-up height, starts standing on it. One that appears higher up falls. The scenario's rules on where bots may appear around your view apply too: on 1w4ts a new target never appears right under your crosshair, and on ww5t new targets appear near where you're looking. Bots heal if the scenario gives them health regen, and lose health on their own if it drains them. Healing comes a step at a time, a tenth of a second's worth of regen per step, as it does in KovaaK's. With no regen delay a step lands every tenth of a second and hits don't pause it. With a delay, each hit holds healing off for that long, then one step lands at once and the rest follow every tenth of a second. A hit on the frame the wait runs out starts the wait again, so a bot whose delay is as long as your shot interval can't heal while you keep hitting it. Draining is steady: on VT Aether and PGT each target goes after 19 seconds and the next one comes. When a scenario lists its bots in a set order, they come in that order (VT Aether's three targets, one after another). Invincible bots (VT Snake Track, PGTI Voltaic) take your hits, which still score, but never go down. A scenario whose bot has a set number of lives ends on the kill that uses the last one: with 3 lives the run is over after your third kill, and each kill is of the next bot in the scenario's order. Lives play when the scenario has one bot slot (the three scenarios checked in KovaaK's all do). Lives on several bots at once, or on a helper, can't be played yet, and the page says so. Bots that can't die have no lives to use up. Some scenarios pay for time: the score gets a number of points for every second still left on the clock when the run ends, so finishing early pays and running out the clock pays nothing. While you play, the score on screen counts the seconds left right now, so it ticks down. Scenarios that multiply the score by damage efficiency scale it by the damage you did over the damage your shots could have done: 3 hits of 4 shots at 10 damage each is 30 of 40, so the score is cut to three quarters. In a KovaaK's scenario a shot that kills counts its whole damage as done, as it does in KovaaK's: a shot of 10 at a bot with 5 health left is 10 done of 10 possible, so it doesn't lower your efficiency, and a scenario that pays points per damage pays for all 10. This changed in November 2026. Before then such a shot counted only the health it took, so scores in KovaaK's scenarios that pay per damage or multiply by damage efficiency can now come out a little higher for the same play, and never lower. Saved scores and bests stay as they are. In the built-in scenarios a shot still counts only the health it takes. With the beam weapon every moment you fire counts as possible damage, on target or off it. When a scenario has several score rules at once (an accuracy multiplier beside damage efficiency, a multiplier beside points for time or kills), the multipliers apply to the whole score, and the page notes that this combination hasn't been checked against KovaaK's. The kill efficiency multiplier can't be played, because no KovaaK's result exists to check a formula against.

**How bots move:** as their dodge profiles say: they strafe left and right, come closer or back off to keep their distance from you, jump, fly up and down or use a jetpack, and change direction when hit or blocked, with their character's speed, acceleration, friction and gravity. A jumping bot works a jump key: every so often it decides whether the key is down until its next decision, and a jump needs a press, so a key held through several decisions gives one jump. A bot that comes down with the key held jumps when it first lands, and some characters jump again at every landing or remember a press made in the air. A jetpack thrusts while the key is held, after its activation delay (when the press is made on the ground) and while its tank has fuel; when the tank runs dry it can't start again for a quarter of a second. A flier either swaps between up and down on a timer or, when its profile says so, flies up while its jump key is down and down while a crouch key is. With no key down it hovers, or falls if its character has gravity. Bots don't walk off ledges. A bot that never moves by itself still falls to the floor when its character has gravity. Bots with movement abilities dash, blink and jump with them: an ability has charges that refill, and a bot tries one when its ability key ticks, if its rules allow it (how far you are, whether it can see you, on the ground or in the air). The key ticks on a timer from the moment the bot appears, in stretches where the bot's willing to press it and stretches where it isn't. A bot waits a set time before it uses anything, a second if its character doesn't say. The wait counts once, from the start of the run: a bot that comes back after you kill it doesn't wait again. If a file leaves out the key's timer, the key ticks every tenth of a second, and if it leaves out how often the bot presses it, the bot always does. When a bot comes back, its abilities' reuse times and its willing and unwilling stretches run on through the death as if nothing had happened, while the new bot gets its charges back and a fresh tick timer. That was measured in KovaaK's with test maps. A few details weren't measured, and are played like this: a delay after a use and the pause shared by all of a bot's abilities start again with each new bot, a dash or blink going on when its bot dies ends with it, and a reaction to damage that is waiting, with the damage counted toward one, is dropped. Each ability has a reuse time that spaces its tries: a tick counts as a try only when more than that time has passed since the ability's last try, even if that try found no charge ready, so a tick of 0.1 s and a reuse time of 0.1 s give a try every 0.2 s. A charge starts to refill when it's used, however long the dash lasts, and one that comes back at the very moment of a try waits for the next one. The same goes for the end of a dash and of an ability's delay. That timing was measured in KovaaK's. An ability can cost the bot health: every use takes the cost off the bot's health at once. That isn't a hit, so it adds nothing to the damage numbers. A bot that spends its last health this way is counted as a target that went away, like one that drains away on its own: no kill, no score and no damage. It comes back after its respawn delay at full health. (Any bot comes back after its respawn delay, and when a file gives a range of delays that's the shortest, as measured in KovaaK's.) Your shots still kill a bot the cost has weakened, and the damage you did counts as usual. Two combinations haven't been checked in KovaaK's, and a scenario with one says so on its page. On a bot whose health regenerates or drains, the cost is played as not delaying that. With bot lives, a bot that dies of its cost is played as using up no life. A dash or blink that can go up or down takes its slope from the bot's aim line. KovaaK's runs that line from one body height above the bot's middle to one body height above the middle of your character (measured there with test maps). A dash set to the movement keys heads where the bot's keys point and climbs or drops as steeply as that line, so a bot above you blinks downward and one below you blinks upward, whichever way it strafes. A flying bot that blinks to and fro drifts, blink by blink, to the height where the line is level. A dash that isn't set to the movement keys goes along the line itself, toward you for a positive speed and away for a negative one. Two cases aren't played, because nobody has measured them: a dash that keeps its gravity while it lasts, and a bot or player shaped like a box. A few rarer combinations aren't played either, and a scenario's page names what blocks it. A bot can also use a dash or blink when you hurt it. Once the damage it has taken since its last reaction reaches the ability's threshold, the bot may ignore that by chance, and otherwise it uses the ability after a short random wait. Damage that reaches the threshold again during the wait starts the wait over. When the wait ends the ability needs a charge, and its delay since its last use must be over. If not, the reaction is dropped and doesn't wait for the charge. The reuse time and the key's ticks play no part, so a bot that never presses its key uses the ability only this way. A reaction counts hits on a bot that can't be hurt too, with your weapon's damage. That was measured in KovaaK's with test maps. A few details weren't measured, and are played like this: the damage counted is forgotten once no hit has landed for the ability's reset time, a reaction that comes due while an ability is going on is dropped, the ability's own rules and the wait at the start of the run hold a reaction back, and when a tick of the key and a reaction fall due at the same moment, the tick goes first. A reaction on an ability with no duration, a single impulse, still isn't played. Healing abilities and a cost on a bot that can't die aren't played yet either, so scenarios with them stay unplayable. A bot that stays put can carry one weapon ability whose weapon blows the bot up, which gives each target a time limit of its own. The bot fires on its ability key's ticks like it uses any ability, and the weapon waits its time between shots: a press the weapon can't answer within a tenth of a second is lost. A blast goes off a set time after each shot and takes health off the bot. A bot that dies of its own blast is counted as a target that went away, like one that spends its last health on an ability: no kill, no score and no damage, and it comes back after its respawn delay. Shoot it first and it's an ordinary kill. The weapon's wait between shots runs on through a death, so the next bot fires when it would have anyway, while the key's ticks and the charges start again. That was measured in KovaaK's with test maps. A few details weren't measured, and are played like this: a press the weapon doesn't answer still uses up the ability's charge, and a blast still on its way when its bot is killed is dropped. A scenario with bot lives says on its page that a bot that blows itself up uses up no life, and that hasn't been checked in KovaaK's. Every other weapon ability still doesn't play, and the scenario's page names the first setting that stops it. A map can hold waypoints for bots to move along. A bot follows them only when its dodge profile says so and a spawn point names a path, as measured in KovaaK's with test maps, so waypoints that no bot follows change nothing here. A bot that does follow a path doesn't play yet, and the scenario's page says so. The one exception is a follower with a top speed of 0 that nothing else moves (no flight, jump, jetpack or dash): in KovaaK's it stayed where it appeared, and it does here. A map can also hold hurt volumes and teleporters. A hurt volume shows as a see-through purple box, as it does in KovaaK's. A bot that touches one is killed, or loses health each time the volume's cooldown runs out, as measured in KovaaK's with test maps. A bot that dies of one goes away as a timeout, with no kill and no score for you, and your shots pass through the box. A teleporter plays only when all it does is send bots into a volume that kills them at once: you can't see it, and a bot that touches it is gone on the spot. The rest doesn't play yet, and the scenario's page names what stops it: a teleporter that moves a bot somewhere it lives or that waits before it does, a volume that is turned, a volume where you start, volumes in a scenario with bots that can't die or bots on your side, and jump pads. The touch was measured with ball bots only, and a scenario with volumes says so on its page. A weapon that fires several pellets a click, like a shotgun, plays when it doesn't spread while you stand still. As measured in KovaaK's with test maps, a click is one shot and one hit however many pellets it has, every pellet lands on the first bot your crosshair meets, and the click does the damage of all of them. Pellets against a bot with a head weren't checked there, and a scenario with both says so on its page. A weapon whose shots spread while you stand still doesn't play yet, with one pellet or many, because how the spread grows wasn't measured. Neither does a pellet count that isn't a whole number. Scenarios can mix several kinds of bot, each with its own size, health, color and movement, and bots can come in clusters. This is modelled on the settings and isn't checked against KovaaK's yet, so moving targets may feel a little different there.

**Bot shapes:** a round bot is a ball however tall its file says it is, a cylinder bot is a capsule (rounded ends) and a box bot is a box. A box stays lined up with the map's axes, so box bots play only when you start the scenario facing along one of them. Cylinder and box bots flatter than they are wide don't play yet, because KovaaK's draws them in a way that isn't understood.

**Heads:** a cylinder or round bot can have a head, a ball on top of its body with its own size, drawn in the head colour. Body and head are centred on the bot's middle, and a head can sink into the body or float above it with a gap that shots pass through. A shot hits one bot at most, and whichever part it meets first counts. A weapon that can headshot does its headshot multiplier times the damage to the head, and the scenario's damage efficiency counts that full headshot damage as possible on every shot, even when no bot has a head. A bot marked headshots only takes nothing on its body: a hit there counts as a miss and doesn't hold back its health regen. Aim at the head of a bot like that. A bot with a head stands on the floor with the bottom of its body, so it is as tall as body and head together. The body can be a speck under its head, smaller than 1 cm, where a body with no head still needs 1 cm. A speck like that can start with its body and head inside a block of the map, because the head makes it taller than the room under its spawn point. KovaaK's was measured in two cases: the bot ends up standing on the block, or against a wall that stands flush with the block's edge. The trainer plays those two, counting the bot's fixed spawn offset first as KovaaK's does. A file that puts such a bot inside a wall in any other way doesn't play, with the reason on its page, and neither does one whose range of spawn offsets reaches into a wall. A bot with a wider body under its head starts where it always did. All of that was measured in KovaaK's with test maps. A few details weren't measured. The body of a headshots-only bot stops a shot meant for a bot behind it, because a shot stops at the first thing it meets. Two more are noted on a scenario's page when it uses them: a head sunk deeper than twice its radius, and a head wider than its body, which bumps into walls and other bots at the width of the body. Heads on box bots, a head with no size, headshots only on a character with no head or with a weapon that can't headshot, and a bot that blows itself up and has a head don't play.

**Walls:** your shots stop at walls, and bots bump into them. Invisible walls (clips) keep bots in but let your shots through, as in KovaaK's. Cylinders and tubes (round arenas), ramps, wedges and curved walls are solid, shaped and placed as in KovaaK's (checked side by side on DriftTS, ww5t, ControlTS and Frogtagon). A few rarer shapes, such as spheres, cones and stairs, are drawn from their names, so they may look a little different from KovaaK's, and shots and bots pass through them. You see the map from the same height as in KovaaK's (measured there with test maps): you start a little above the scenario's spawn point, moved by your character's own spawn offsets if it has them, and stand on the floor if you'd start inside it or just above it, however small your character is. Bots start at the same height as there too.

**Your runs:** runs played in AimAdapt are kept apart from your KovaaK's runs of the same scenario. They don't change your KovaaK's bests, trends or benchmark ranks. The card shows how many you've played in AimAdapt and your best.

**What's still different:** the card lists what plays differently from KovaaK's, for example walls that are drawn from their names, or a walking bot that crouches, which plays standing up. The sizes and distances still have to be checked against KovaaK's, so a score here may not match a score there yet.

## Replay {#replay}

Your recent runs in the trainer can be watched again. On Scenarios, pick a scenario and click **Replay** next to a run in its **Runs**. **Back to** returns to the scenario. Straight after a run, **V** on the trainer's results screen opens its replay.

The replay shows your own view: the crosshair stays in the middle and the view turns with it, as it did in the game, at the field of view you played at. The faint dots are a fixed grid, so you can see the view move even with no targets near.

- **Targets** are drawn where they were, at their size. A target lights up while your crosshair is on it, and a white ring shows its health once it's been damaged.
- **Trail:** a green line shows where the crosshair has been in the last 0.4 s, so you can see overshoot and corrections. Turn it off with **Trail**.
- **Shots:** a white ring for a hit and a red cross for a miss, where the crosshair was. The ticks under the bar mark every shot, red for misses, so you can jump to them.
- **Totals** in the corner count kills, shots and hit rate up to that moment. Tracking runs show kills and whether you're on target.
- **Controls:** **Play**/**Pause**, the bar to jump anywhere, and **Speed** (0.25× to 2×). Keys: Space plays or pauses, ← and → skip a second (with Shift, five), `,` and `.` step one frame, Home and End go to the start and the end.

A replay is redrawn from what the run recorded: your aim 240 times a second and the targets 60 times a second. It isn't a video, so it takes little space and records nothing outside the trainer. Runs from before replays were added, and KovaaK's runs, have none.

**Which runs keep one:** each scenario keeps the replays of its latest 50 runs and of your best run (the first to reach your top score, or your best rating in adaptive mode; after a change to the scenario's settings, the best on each version). When an older run drops out of the 50, only its replay goes: its rating, score and insights stay.

## Settings {#settings}

Settings has four tabs: **Aim and display**, **Training**, **KovaaK's** and **Your data**.

**Aim and display** holds your profiles. Each profile has its own sensitivity, DPI, field of view, crosshair and display settings, typically one per game. The trainer plays with the active one (Scenarios shows which); **Use this one** makes the selected profile active. **Add**, **Duplicate** and **Delete** manage them. Changes aren't used until you press **Save settings**; **Undo changes** throws them away.

**Aim**
- **Game:** pick the game, then type your sensitivity exactly as it is in that game. AimAdapt converts it to cm/360 at your DPI. Choose cm/360 to enter it directly.
- **Mouse DPI.**
- **Field of view:** horizontal, at 16:9. Where a game uses another FOV measure, **Use** fills in the matching value.
- **Match my KovaaK's settings** copies the sensitivity, DPI and FOV of your latest KovaaK's run.

**Crosshair:** color, arm length (0 for a dot only), thickness, gap and a center dot, with a preview at actual size. **Show FPS** turns on the counter (F2 toggles it in game).

**Display**
- **Mode:** Borderless (full screen at your desktop resolution), Exclusive full screen (any resolution and refresh rate your monitor supports, for example stretched 4:3) or Window.
- **Resolution:** for a window, its size. For exclusive full screen, the screen mode.
- **Refresh rate:** exclusive full screen only. Leave it empty for the highest.
- **Play area:** draws the game in a smaller area with black around it, so a big monitor plays like a smaller one. **Set play area** works it out from the screen size you want and your monitor's size; **Use whole screen** turns it off.
- **Play area position:** centered, at the top or bottom of the screen, or a custom spot (how far from the left and from the top, as a percentage of the black space). The preview shows where it will be.

Sensitivity is in degrees per mouse count, so it stays the same at any resolution or play-area size.

**Training** holds what shapes your practice. Each change is saved as you make it.
- **Adaptive target mix:** Steady (mostly near your rating), Balanced (some stretch and easy runs) or Push (more stretch runs). One mix holds for every profile. If your profiles had mixes of their own, the active profile's carries over, and your first change on this tab sets it for every profile. See [Adaptive mode](#help/adaptive).
- **Daily goal:** minutes of active time a day, from 1 to 600. It starts at 30. Today, your streak and the daily-time chart use it, and the weekly goal is seven times it.
- **How long to train:** half an hour a day is a sound default, and nothing suggests it is a ceiling. In the largest study of aim training (Listman and others, 2021, on about 7,000 Aim Lab players and one task), gains kept growing up to about an hour of that task in a day, with most of the benefit reached within 30 to 60 minutes. Training on more days mattered more than the length of any one day.

How time is counted is fixed for now:
- A new session starts after a 15-minute break.
- Session time runs from a session's first run to its last, so it includes menus and breaks. Active time is time inside runs.
- The training day starts at 4 AM.
- A run under half the usual length of its scenario counts as restarted, and never as a best.

**KovaaK's:** AimAdapt finds KovaaK's in any of your Steam libraries and imports its per-run stats files (FPSAimTrainer\stats). It only reads them and never changes your KovaaK's files. The tab shows the stats folder, how many files it found and imported, any files that couldn't be read, and **Rescan folder**. **Sensitivity history** lists each period with its sensitivity, DPI, FOV, cm/360 and run count. If your FPS sits at KovaaK's cap, it suggests raising the cap.

**Your data** shows where your database and your own scenarios and workouts are (see [Your data](#help/data)).

## Your data {#data}

Everything is stored on your computer in `%APPDATA%\AimAdapt`:
- `aimadapt.db`: your runs and ratings (SQLite).
- `engine.toml`: your settings profiles. If AimAdapt can't read it, Settings tells you and shows the defaults, and saving keeps the old file as `engine (unusable).toml` so nothing in it is lost.
- `training.toml`: the adaptive mix and the daily goal from Settings › Training.
- `scenarios\` and `workouts\`: your own scenario and workout files.
- `crash.log`: only there if AimAdapt has crashed. It says what went wrong, which helps with a bug report.

AimAdapt used to be called RustyAim. The first time the new version runs, it moves your old `%APPDATA%\RustyAim` folder to `%APPDATA%\AimAdapt`, so your history, ratings, profiles, scenarios and workouts carry over.

Nothing is sent anywhere. Deleting one of your scenarios or workouts removes the file; your past runs stay in your history.

## Troubleshooting {#troubleshooting}

- **The mouse isn't locked to the trainer.** It locks again by itself within half a second of the trainer getting focus back. If it doesn't, click inside the trainer window.
- **A rating started over.** The scenario's settings changed, so it calibrates again. This happens when you edit a scenario, and when an update changes a built-in.
- **A calibration run on a scenario you've played before.** You were away from it for a few days, so the trainer finds your level again, starting from your old rating (see [Adaptive mode](#help/adaptive)).
- **No KovaaK's runs.** Settings › KovaaK's shows where AimAdapt looked. It searches every Steam library for FPSAimTrainer\stats. Use **Rescan folder** after installing KovaaK's.
- **The trainer opens in the wrong place.** It opens over the app window, so move the app window where you want the trainer, or use full screen.
- **The trainer closed straight away.** The app shows why in a banner, such as a scenario file it couldn't load.
- **"Couldn't load data" when the app opens.** The message says why. If your history was saved by a newer version of AimAdapt, update AimAdapt to open it; the older version won't write to it.

- A simpler layout: four pages instead of ten. **Today** has today's workout and the week; **Scenarios** lists everything you can play, newest first with filters, beside the selected scenario's page (Play, figures, notes, Insights and runs on one scrolling page); **Progress** has Skills, Benchmarks and Time; **Settings** gains Goals and time, KovaaK's (what was Library & import) and Your data. Old links still go to the right place.
- Replays: watch any new trainer run again from its scenario's page, from your own view, at any speed, with your shots and the crosshair's trail.
- The Reaction column in a scenario's runs table leaves out flicks under 3°, like the Reaction time chart, so the two agree in Micro Flicks.
- The play area can sit anywhere on the screen, for example near the top, instead of always centered.
- The mouse locks to the trainer again by itself after alt-tabbing.
- Hop Strafe Track, Unbroken Strafes, Hop Switch and Hop Clicks strafe further side to side. Their ratings start with a new calibration run.
- Target speeds show one decimal (48.5 °/s), so small changes are visible.
- RustyAim is now AimAdapt. Your data moves to the new folder on the first run.
- A user guide in the app (this page).
- Each drill is rated on what it trains: kill pace for Static Grid, Timed Grid and Wide Flicks; target size for Micro Flicks and Precise Track; target speed for Strafe Clicks. These scenarios start with a new calibration run.
- Control Track moves in real curves.
- Insights adds centering while on target, and on lifetime ratings, the share that's reaction time.
- The trainer opens over the app window.
- Targets move more like players: they slow down and speed back up at turns, come closer and back away, and jump. Ten new scenarios use this, and most Core Week drills alternate with one of them week by week.
- Adaptive mode calibrates on your first run, then plays each run at one difficulty, with stretch and easy runs and breakthroughs.
- Switching is rated on what it trains: kill pace for Speed Switch, and time on target while you're on a kill for Stability Switch and Switch Track.
